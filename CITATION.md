# Citing KOVA3

## Citing the dataset

When you use KOVA3, cite the resource **and state the release version**.
Allele frequencies change between releases; an analysis is not reproducible
without the version.

```
Korean Variant Archive 3 (KOVA3), release vX.Y.Z.
Choi Lab, Korea University College of Medicine.
Registry of Open Data on AWS: https://registry.opendata.aws/kova3/ (live at launch)
```

A Citation File Format entry is provided at the repository root
([CITATION.cff](CITATION.cff)); GitHub uses it to render a **"Cite this
repository"** button. It already carries the Registry of Open Data URL. The
`version` and `date-released` fields, and a dataset DOI, are added at first
release.

## Citing the predecessors

KOVA3 builds on two earlier Korean variant frequency resources. If your work
draws on the KOVA lineage rather than only the current release, cite these as
well.

- **KOVA**: Lee S, Seo J, Park J, Nam J-Y, Choi A, Ignatius JS, Bjornson RD,
  Chae J-H, Jang I-J, Lee S, Park W-Y, Baek D, Choi M. *Korean Variant Archive
  (KOVA): a reference database of genetic variations in the Korean population.*
  Scientific Reports 2017;7(1):4287.
  doi:[10.1038/s41598-017-04642-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-04642-4)
- **KOVA2**: Lee J, Lee J, Jeon S, Lee J, Jang I, Yang JO, Park S, Lee B,
  Choi J, Choi B-O, Gee HY, Oh J, Jang I-J, Lee S, Baek D, Koh Y, Yoon S-S,
  Kim Y-J, Chae J-H, Park W-Y, Bhak JH, Choi M. *A database of 5305 healthy
  Korean individuals reveals genetic and clinical implications for an East Asian
  population.* Experimental & Molecular Medicine 2022;54(11):1862-1871; 1,896
  whole genomes and 3,409 whole exomes.
  doi:[10.1038/s12276-022-00871-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s12276-022-00871-4)

## Acknowledging the contributing cohorts

KOVA3 aggregates data generated by several Korean genome projects. If your
work depends on a particular cohort, cite that cohort's own publication
alongside KOVA3. Two of the contributing cohorts have one.

- **Korea4K**: Jeon S, Choi H, Jeon Y, et al. *Korea4K: whole genome sequences
  of 4,157 Koreans with 107 phenotypes derived from extensive health check-ups.*
  GigaScience 2024;13.
  doi:[10.1093/gigascience/giae014](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giae014)
- **Korea10K**: An K, Jeon S, Kwon Y, et al. *10,239 whole genomes with
  multiomic and clinical health information as the Korean population multiomic
  reference dataset.* bioRxiv 2025.
  doi:[10.1101/2025.11.17.688763](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.11.17.688763)
  (preprint, not peer reviewed; replace with the journal version once published)

Both were produced by the Korean Genomics Center (KOGIC) at UNIST.
